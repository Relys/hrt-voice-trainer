Vowel Chart
Plots your live F1/F2 (the same two formants shown in the HUD) as a moving dot. Two reference modes: "IPA vowels" (IPA vowels, Hillenbrand et al. 1995) shows which vowel shape your tongue/jaw are making; "M/F clusters" instead shows your position against masculine- and feminine-typical corner-vowel averages (Peterson & Barney 1952 / Hillenbrand et al. 1995) — population averages with substantial individual overlap, not a category your voice must fall into. Approximate, not diagnostic either way.
Press Start, sustain an /i/ ("ee") vowel, then tap Calibrate — Hirsch's technique carries that tongue position's F2 across other vowels to brighten resonance consistently. The dashed line on the chart above shows your calibrated F2.

Settings

Switches the surrounding app's light/dark appearance. The spectrogram and vowel chart canvases always stay dark regardless — the color ramp is designed against black, same as any video/image viewer.
Frequency axis spacing. Log (default) matches how pitch is actually perceived — equal ratios read as equal steps, like piano octaves. Linear spaces frequencies evenly in Hz instead, compressing low-voice-range detail toward the bottom.
Palette mapping loudness to color on both spectrogram views. Viridis (default) is perceptually-uniform and colorblind-safe; Rainbow matches the classic Chrome Music Lab look; Hot is a black-to-white thermal ramp; Grayscale is the traditional Praat-style look.
Highest frequency shown on the vertical axis. Lower ranges zoom into the fundamental/formant region; higher ranges also show the 2.8-3.5kHz ring/twang band and sibilants, at the cost of compressing the low range visually.
Trade-off between time and frequency precision in the underlying analysis window. Fine gives sharper frequency detail but blurs fast changes over time; Fast tracks quick changes better but blurs frequency detail; Balanced splits the difference.
How many resonances the formant estimator (Linear Predictive Coding) tries to fit at once. Higher can resolve closely-spaced formants but risks fitting spurious peaks to noise; lower is more stable but may miss a real formant. 12 is a solid default for adult speech.
How many past time-slices the 3D waterfall keeps visible at once (its scrolling depth). Longer shows more history but uses more GPU memory and processing.
Vertical size of the spectrogram, frequency ruler, and 3D view. Taller gives more frequency resolution on screen; shorter fits more on a small display.
Upper bound of the color ramp, in dB. Loudness at or above this level renders at the top color of the scheme — lower this if loud passages all look the same flat color.
Lower bound of the color ramp, in dB. Loudness at or below this renders as black/silence. Raising this mutes quiet background noise, but how high depends on your specific mic and room.

Boosts or cuts the live mic signal before analysis and recording — use this to compensate for a quiet mic, or pull down a hot one that's clipping. Applies to live capture only, not clip playback.
Shades a horizontal band across the pitch range you're aiming for, and lights up the Pitch readout when you're inside it. Presets approximate ASHA's speaking-fundamental-frequency guidance — not clinical cutoffs. Custom lets you set your own Hz range below.

Raw microphone input level in dB, shown instantly with no smoothing or gating — use it to check you're loud enough without clipping. It's a gain-staging aid, not an acoustic-quality measure.
Fundamental frequency (F0), estimated with the YIN algorithm from how strongly the waveform matches a delayed copy of itself. The note name is the nearest musical pitch.
Draws a moving blue dot on the spectrogram/3D view at your live pitch position. Independent of the Pitch readout above — turn either off without affecting the other.
How far your pitch is from your Target range band, in Hz — negative means below the band, positive means above it, zero means you're inside it. A companion to "% in target" (History/Progress): that's a strict hit/miss count, this shows which direction to adjust and by how much. Only meaningful when a Target range is set in Settings.
How much your pitch moves around, not just where it sits — the standard deviation of pitch (in semitones) over roughly the last few seconds of voiced speech. Low = monotone, high = varied/expressive.
Cepstral Peak Prominence — a standard clinical measure of vocal "weight"/breathiness (Hillenbrand & Houde, 1996). A clean, resonant voice has a sharp cepstral peak; a breathy voice has a flatter one. Higher dB = fuller, less breathy.
Harmonic-to-Noise Ratio in dB — how strongly the waveform correlates with a shifted copy of itself at your pitch period. Periodic, clear voice scores high; breathy or noisy voice scores low.
Frame-to-frame percent change in pitch period (jitter) and amplitude (shimmer) — approximate perturbation indicators, useful as a trend. True clinical jitter/shimmer are measured cycle-by-cycle on individual glottal pulses; this is a coarser ~10ms-frame approximation, not a diagnostic number.
Percent of total spectral energy in the 2.8-3.5 kHz "singer's formant" band. More energy there reads as a brighter, more forward, ringing quality.
Numeric F1/F2 readout in the HUD above. F1 relates mostly to jaw/tongue height, F2 to tongue front/back position — together they shape vowel quality and perceived resonance.
Third vocal-tract resonance (formant), estimated via Linear Predictive Coding. Contributes to "ring"/timbre quality. Noisier frame-to-frame than F1/F2 — treat it as a coarser trend, not a precise readout.
Draws pink (F1) and gold (F2) dots on the spectrogram/3D view at their live frequency position. Independent of the F1/F2 readout above.
Mean of whichever formants (F1/F2/F3) are currently detected — a single rough resonance-brightness indicator.
Numeric nearest-vowel readout in the HUD above, from the same F1/F2 match used by the Vowel Chart panel.
Off by default. Shows a live pitch/formant-based acoustic-similarity score against masculine/feminine reference averages, plus a session trend and breakdown pie chart. See the panel's own info bubble for the exact formula and its limits — it is not a gender or "passing" prediction.

Welcome

A few optional things to set up before you start — everything here can be changed anytime in Settings, and nothing here ever leaves your device.

1. Target range

Pick a pitch range to aim for — unlocks the "% in target", Target Δ, and Acoustic Proximity target-matching features.

2. Recording

Stored locally in your browser only — nothing is uploaded anywhere. Off by default.

3. Microphone

Pick which input to use — this also sets the main Microphone selector.

4. Background noise calibration

Stay quiet for a few seconds so we can measure your room/mic's actual noise floor and set it automatically, instead of guessing with a slider.
